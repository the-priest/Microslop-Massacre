# HELLO, FRIEND

### An open-world RPG in the world of *Mr. Robot*, played like *Fallout: New Vegas*

> *"Hello, friend. That's lame. Maybe I should give you a name. But that's a slippery slope."*

You are Elliot Alderson: cybersecurity engineer by day, vigilante hacker by night,
and not always sure which of those is the real you. A man in an old jacket says he's
been waiting for you your whole life. E Corp owns seventy percent of the world's
consumer debt. The city is one long, rainy, neon-lit argument about who gets to be free.

Walk anywhere. Talk to anyone. Pick the lock, or pick a fight. Steal a car, or a plane.
Decide how the biggest company on Earth falls, and who you become while it does.

**Free. Original. No assets downloaded, nothing licensed, nothing ripped: every street,
character, sound and song is built in code when the game starts.**

## What it is

A first-person open-world RPG that takes the systems of *Fallout: New Vegas* (skills,
perks, traits, V.A.T.S., factions, companions, branching quests, a talking epilogue)
and drops them into the New York of *Mr. Robot*.

- **14 endings**, from blowing up the world's debt with fsociety to walking away and
  going to therapy on Tuesdays. Some never touch the hack at all.
- **61 epilogue slides** that remember what you did: who you saved, who you sold out,
  whose debt you deleted, whether Lopez ever caught you.
- **20 quests** (7 main, 13 side), each with several real outcomes, and a story director
  that keeps every quest moving no matter what order you do things in.
- **82 named characters**, about 25,000 words of original dialogue, and honest skill
  checks: a white `[SPEECH 40]` means you pass.
- **Four companions** with their own skills, opinions, barks, heart-to-hearts and
  breaking points.
- **Every parked car can be stolen. There's an airfield. The planes fly.**

It's a fan project: an original story that runs alongside the show rather than
retelling it. No scripts from the series are reproduced.

## The city

One seamless map, no loading between districts: the Lower East Side, Chinatown, the
Civic Center, Midtown and Turtle Bay, Hell's Kitchen, the Upper East Side, Central Park,
Washington Heights, Inwood and Fort Tryon Park, Harlem, the Bronx, Astoria, Long Island
City, Hunts Point, the Bowery Bay Airfield, Steel Mountain, and Coney Island at the
bottom of it all, with the Twin Towers still standing.

- **Day and night, rain and fog.** Streets go wet in the rain. Neon flickers. Stores close.
- **A living street.** Pedestrians cross at the corners; traffic keeps to its lanes,
  waits at lights and honks at you.
- **Every building has a door, and thousands of them open:** apartments, bodegas,
  pawn shops, diners, dive bars, offices, warehouses, squats and gang hideouts. Each is
  generated from its address, so the same door always leads to the same rooms.
- **38 hand-made story interiors**, from Krista's office and Ron's Coffee to the E Corp
  tower, the Fun Society arcade, Steel Mountain, the Rose Garden and a stash house on Pier 9.
- **Everything on the street is searchable:** dumpsters, newsboxes, mailboxes, food carts,
  gloveboxes, shipping containers, hidden stashes.

## The story

The main quest, assembling the **Five/Nine** hack, needs four pieces, and you can chase
them in almost any order:

- **The Rootkit.** Ron's Coffee runs a stalkerware business out of its back room.
  Report it, expose it, take the customer list, or just plant fsociety's rootkit and walk.
- **Steel Mountain.** E Corp keeps its analog backups in a mountain. Talk your way in,
  sneak in wearing coveralls, or shoot your way to the climate controls.
- **An ally.** Earn the Dark Army's help through Whiterose, or feed the FBI just enough
  to make them look at the wrong screens.
- **The inside man.** Turn Tyrell Wellick, or take an E Corp keycard the hard way.

Then a table at the Salina Hotel, the people who own the world, and the last choice.

**The endings:** Five/Nine · Stage Two · Clean Hands · The Board Seat · Reboot ·
Press Freedom · Mr. Robot · The Monster · The Kingpin · The Quiet Life · The Confession ·
The Informant · The Company Man · and the hidden **Overlap**, which only opens if you
built the right bonds, kept the truth, and held your mind together.

**Side stories,** each with several endings of their own:

| Quest | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Sugar** | The Bronx | A dealer called Candyman is selling cut "candy" that kills kids. Hand his ledger to the police, burn his lab, turn his suppliers on him, make him retire, or take his corners yourself. |
| **Bad Company** | Harlem / Astoria | A youth coach is grooming a boy at a shelter. Get proof; get him stopped. (Never graphic: the story is about protecting the kid.) |
| **Wings** | Bowery Bay Airfield | An old Air Force mechanic is about to lose his airfield to E Corp's finance arm. Fly his ring course, land it in one piece, then save the field. |
| **The Detective** | The 7th Precinct | Kill enough innocent people and Detective Lopez starts drawing your pattern on a board. |
| **The Leverage** | Your building, the docks | Shayla, your neighbor, owes the wrong man. Get her out of his cage. |
| **Small Disaster** | Krista's life | Krista's new boyfriend Lenny is lying to her. Decide which mercy is real. |
| **Washington Township** | Allsafe | The memos that prove E Corp knew. Who gets to see them is up to you. |
| **No Heat / Street Sermon / Lost / Dead Drops** | All over | A boiler held hostage by a subscription, a busker who needs a signal, a lost dog, packages nobody opens. |

Every objective says where to go and how, and the compass points at it. When an
objective is done, Mr. Robot slides in on a green CRT card to tell you so (and to
tell you when you level up).

## Companions

Recruit up to two at once. They follow you everywhere, fight beside you, ride along in
your car or plane, and talk: about the places you take them, the things you do, and,
once they trust you, themselves.

| Companion | Where | With you | After three heart-to-hearts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Darlene** | The arcade (fsociety) | +10 HACKING | *Family Business*: +5 HACKING, for good |
| **Leon** | Coney Island | +10 GUNS; hands you a sandwich when you're dying | *The Number Four*: +5 GUNS |
| **Trenton** | The arcade (fsociety) | +10 SNEAK, +5 SPEECH | *Keep the Number*: +5 SPEECH |
| **Shayla** | Your building (once she's free) | +10 MEDICINE, +5 BARTER, and something from her bag every day | *Neighbor*: +5 MEDICINE |

Heart-to-hearts open up the longer you travel together. Companions react to your big
choices, and each has a line they won't follow you over: sell out fsociety, kill
people who had nothing to do with it, or turn the corners into a business, and some of
them will walk out and not come back.

## Cars and planes

**Cars.** Every parked car in the city can be stolen: walk up and press **E**. A good
LOCKPICK slips the lock quietly; otherwise it's an elbow through the window, which
people notice. Carjack anything stopped in traffic, police cruisers included if you
want the whole NYPD on you. Arcade handling, a handbrake, a swinging chase camera, a
horn that scatters crowds. Crashes hurt the car (smoke, then fire, then it goes up).
Running people over is a crime, and the Monster path is watching.

**Planes.** Bowery Bay Airfield sits on the Queens waterfront in the north-east corner of
the map: one runway, a control tower, two hangars, three Skyhawk trainers and E Corp's
corporate jet. Fly Gus's ring course and he'll let you take a trainer whenever you like.
The jet you'll have to steal.

- Full throttle down the runway; pull back once she's light.
- Bank to turn. Don't get slow: below stall speed the wing quits and the nose drops.
- Land gently, wings level and nose a hair up, and you can fly again. Land hard and you won't.
- Instruments on the HUD: airspeed, altitude, throttle, heading, and STALL / PULL UP warnings.
- Fly anywhere over the city, from the Bronx to the Coney Island boardwalk.

## Making a living

- **Job board** (phone → DATA → JOBS): new contracts every day: deliveries, bounties,
  office data heists, repo jobs, hits on gang hideouts.
- **ATMs:** hack the cash cassette, or cash out stolen cards.
- **Pickpocketing:** crouch behind anyone and press E.
- **Gloveboxes:** steal a car and whatever's in the glovebox comes with it.
- **Barter:** hundreds of shops, each with its own stock and its own cash. Night dealers
  fence what the pawn shops won't touch.
- **Street encounters:** muggings in progress (stop them, get paid), ambushes,
  shakedowns, dropped wallets.
- **35 weapons**, including 8 hidden uniques: White Hat, Zero Day, Kernel Panic,
  Patch Tuesday, The Daemon, Honeypot, Root Kit and Dead Man's Switch.

## Controls

```
WASD / mouse      move and look            E              interact / talk / take / drive / get out
Shift             sprint                   Left / right   shoot / aim
Ctrl or C         sneak (toggle)           R              reload
Space             jump                     V or Q         EXPLOIT mode
F                 flashlight               H              quick-heal
1 - 8             hotkeyed weapons / aid   Mouse wheel    switch weapon
TAB               phone (stats / level up) I / J / M      phone: items / data / map
T                 wait / sleep             F5 / F9        quicksave / quickload
ESC               pause, save, load, settings

In a car          W/S gas and brake, A/D steer, Space handbrake, G horn, mouse looks around
In a plane        W/S throttle, A/D bank, mouse back (or ↓) climbs, mouse forward (or ↑) dives,
                  mouse left/right rudder, Space wheel brakes, E get out once stopped
```

Every key is listed in-game under **Controls** in the main and pause menus.

## Gamepad

Plug in an Xbox, PlayStation or Steam Deck controller and it just works, everywhere:
the title screen, every menu, dialogue, the phone, the map, terminals, minigames,
barter and the level-up screen. The hints on screen switch to pad buttons the moment
you touch the stick.

```
Left stick        move / steer / bank and pitch      Right stick   look (aim friction on targets)
RT / LT           fire / aim                         A             interact, select
B                 phone, back                        X             reload, take all
Y                 jump                               LB            EXPLOIT
RB                quick-heal                         L3 / R3       sprint / sneak
D-pad             flashlight, wait, switch weapons   START / BACK  pause / map

Car               RT gas, LT brake, X handbrake, LB horn, A get out
Plane             RT/LT throttle, stick banks and pitches (pull back to climb), LB/RB rudder,
                  X brakes, A get out once stopped
Phone             LB/RB tabs, LT/RT sections; on the map the stick picks a place, A travels
EXPLOIT           stick picks target and body part, A queues, X executes, Y undoes, B cancels
Minigames         A plays, B walks away
```

Vibration, look sensitivity and Y-inversion are in **Settings**.

**If the pad moves the mouse cursor instead of the menus:** Steam's *Desktop* controller
layout is turning your pad into a mouse. Add the game to Steam (*Add a Non-Steam Game*)
and launch it from there, or turn Steam Input off for it, and the game gets the real
controller. PlayStation pads on Linux also register a separate "Motion Sensors" device;
the game ignores it automatically.

## System requirements and performance

Made for modest hardware. The target machine is a **Ryzen 5 PRO 4650U laptop (Vega
integrated graphics) with 8 GB of RAM**, and the game runs well there.

- Godot 4 **GL Compatibility** renderer (OpenGL 3.3 / OpenGL ES 3.0), so integrated
  GPUs and older cards are fine.
- The whole city is merged into about five draw calls per 200 m chunk; a typical street
  view is 40–70 draw calls.
- Uses a fraction of an 8 GB RAM budget.

Everything is tunable in **Settings**: 3D resolution scale, view distance, crowd and
traffic density, shadows, MSAA, glow, CRT scanlines, frame cap, FOV, sensitivity,
volumes, and whether to skip the intro.

For a 4650U-class laptop: **Normal view distance, 0.85 resolution scale.**

## Playing it

Grab the latest build from **Releases**:

- **Windows:** unzip and run `HelloFriend.exe`. SmartScreen may warn about an unsigned
  indie executable: click *More info → Run anyway*.
- **Linux:** unzip, then `chmod +x HelloFriend.x86_64` and run it.

Saves, settings and the endings you've unlocked live in Godot's user data folder
(`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\HELLO, FRIEND` on Windows,
`~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/HELLO, FRIEND` on Linux).

## Building from source

1. Install **Godot 4.7** (the standard build, not .NET) from
   [godotengine.org](https://godotengine.org/download), plus its export templates.
2. Clone this repository.
3. Open `project.godot` in the editor and press **Play**, or run it straight from a terminal:

   ```
   godot --path . res://ui/MainMenu.tscn
   ```

4. To export, use the **Linux** and **Windows** presets in `export_presets.cfg`
   (*Project → Export*), or headless:

   ```
   godot --headless --path . --export-release "Linux" build/linux/HelloFriend.x86_64
   ```

   ```
   godot --headless --path . --export-release "Windows" build/windows/HelloFriend.exe
   ```

There is nothing else to install: no external assets, no plugins, no build step.

### Tests

The `test/` folder holds headless test scenes that play the game for you. Run any of
them with `godot --path . res://test/<name>.tscn` (add `--headless`, or run under a
virtual display for the ones that render):

| Scene | What it checks |
|---|---|
| `validate` | Every quest, stage, marker, dialogue jump, effect, item, door, interior, shop and NPC reference resolves, and every quest stage can actually be reached |
| `playthrough`, `story_walk` | The main quest played from the first line to an ending |
| `ending_walk` | One ending per run (set the `ENDING` environment variable) through the dialogue that really offers it |
| `dlgwalk` | Walks every dialogue node |
| `vehicle_test`, `flight_test` | Stealing, driving, crashing, carjacking; taking off, turning, rings, landing, crashing |
| `pad_test`, `menu_pad_test` | The whole game driven by a simulated controller: menus, phone, map, pause, minigames, level-up, dialogue, terminals, cars |
| `companion_test` | Recruiting, following, riding along, heart-to-hearts, reactions, leaving |
| `dd_*`, `smoke`, `intro_test` | Interiors, exploits, the world, the intro |

## Modding the story

Quests and dialogue are plain text. You can rewrite the whole story without touching code.

**Quests** live in `data/quests.qst`:

```
=== sq_wings | Wings | side | 250
One line of description.
10 Find Gus Petrakis in the flight office at Bowery Bay Airfield @npc:gus
20 Fly through Gus's five practice rings over the city @ring
60 done
```

**Dialogue** lives in `data/dialogue/*.dlg`:

```
=== gus
-- start
? q.sq_wings.done -> after
GUS: We're closed. We've been closed since 1987.
* "Teach me to fly." -> teach
* [HACKING 45] "Let me use your computer." -> hack | hack_low
-- teach
! quest sq_wings 20 ; set gus_plane_ok
-> END
```

Lines are spoken lines or `> narration`; `? condition -> node` jumps; `! effect ; effect`
runs effects; `* [if cond] [once] [SKILL dc] text -> pass | fail` are choices. The full
grammar (every condition and effect) is documented at the top of
`autoload/dialogue_manager.gd`. Run `test/validate.tscn` after editing; it tells you
exactly what's broken and where.

## Project layout

```
autoload/    DB (items, perks, skills), GameState, DialogueManager, SaveManager,
             Settings, AudioManager (every sound is synthesized here), SceneRouter,
             Pad (gamepad support)
game/        world generation (city_builder, world_layout, props, furniture,
             interiors), player, NPCs, crowd, traffic, vehicles, aircraft,
             mesh batching, materials, effects, the intro, and the Game controller
ui/          HUD, phone, map, dialogue, loot, barter, level-up, terminals, EXPLOIT,
             the Mr. Robot popup, menus
minigames/   lockpick, terminal exploit, network recon, brute force, signal tap,
             cascade, space invaders, log hunt
shaders/     building facades with procedural windows and neon, sky, water, crowd
             skinning, rain, CRT, glitch and EXPLOIT overlays
data/        quests.qst, dialogue/*.dlg, notes, NPCs, interiors, world objects,
             companions, endings
test/        the headless test scenes above
```
